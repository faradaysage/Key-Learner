## Implemented games

These are separate suite entries in [GameCatalog.cs](Studio/GameCatalog.cs). The IDs below are the persisted `PlayMode` integer values: preserve them or migrate saves explicitly.

| ID / game | Intended and implemented loop |
| --- | --- |
| 0 — **Smash Garden** | Deliberate/rapid typing emits letters or stable key icons. Clusters splat sliding paint; broad overlapping mashing grows glass cracks; tracing emits larger irregular liquid blobs. Includes streaming word recognition and counting. Consecutive repeats inflate/pop a glyph. |
| 1 — **Word Adventure** | Find highlighted keys to spell a dictionary target. Completion immediately announces the word and releases colored balloons. Mouse/cannon play pops them for points and an all-clear bonus/shake, then advances the word. |
| 2 — **Counting Stars** | Type the expected ascending number, including multi-digit numbers, through 100. Each number launches that many fireworks within a fixed roughly four-second launch window; larger numbers do not create proportionally longer waits. |
| 3 — **Sky Speller** | Continuous bird flight collects ordered letters. Up dives, Down climbs/loops, Space boosts; double-tap left/right rolls; Ctrl squawks and helps attract/reveal the next letter. Assist returns toward upright flight above terrain. |
| 4 — **Letter Racer** | Continuous driving with steering, acceleration/braking/boost and the same letter objective. Road shoulders resist departure; off-road travel slows and throws dirt; water is a hard boundary. |
| 5 — **Ocean Speller** | Dolphin swimming with analogous movement, boost/roll and a useful sonar call. Underwater scenery and floor/surface constraints distinguish it from flight. |
| 6 — **Dot Pop** | Brief READY/SET/GO, show 0–9 dots in an invisible 3×3 grid, ask “How many?”, click a large numeral. Correct volleys pop the occupied dots; wrong answers quietly shake and restore the same pattern for an untimed retry. |
| 7 — **How Many Now?** | Show an initial set; dots visibly join or leave; answer the resulting quantity. Show the completed equation and cannon/pop feedback. Advanced equation-first rounds ask before confirming the transformation. |
| 8 — **What's Hiding?** | Show the total, cover part, choose how many are hidden, then reveal the hidden part and its equation. |
| 9 — **Make the Number** | Click empty frame positions to add dots; occupied positions remove them. Automatically complete at the requested target. Later rounds start partly filled to teach missing addends. |
| 10 — **Dot Duel** | Compare structured groups for more/fewer, or choose SAME. Progress toward one, then both groups represented by numerals. |
| 11 — **Cannon Hop** | Predict movement on a 0–10 number track; distinct hops illustrate addition/subtraction. Initially demonstrates before asking. Unlocks after three joining and three separating completions in How Many Now?. |

The three explorers share letter collection, ten points per letter, a word bonus, spoken completion, burst/chime/shake and distinct first/last-letter markers. Bird scenery includes forest, lakes, mountains/valleys, city, river, town and tundra. These games support watching with steering assistance as well as active play. Only ordered word collection is implemented; proposed Scrabble/free-word variants are not.

Word Adventure deliberately does **not** use Smash's fragment expiry or prefix delays. Stray letters preserve the correct prefix; the final target letter completes immediately. The first eight completed words are untimed. Later optional per-letter timing starts at 45 seconds and gradually reaches a 15-second floor. A timeout removes only one letter, shows a gentle correction and waits for another correct input before restarting. Word length increases gradually. Balloon rewards retain the older game's simple score.

Dot Pop samples the 512 occupancy masks, avoids immediate repeats and favors underused patterns/symmetry families. Progression goes from 0–3 to 0–4, structured 5–6, structured 7–9, then irregular 5–9. Display-time limits start only after substantial first-attempt success; **answering itself never times out**. Its stage/mastery is currently session-local.

The five math games reuse the suite instead of introducing a content framework or per-game options. They have eight data-driven levels: quantities within 3; zero/within 5; larger operations within 5; structured then general quantities within 10; gradually hidden support; then equations. Five-frames precede ten-frames. Beginner choices are visual and limited; touchpad targets are large, with no drag, precise aim or rapid-click requirement. Input locks during transformations/feedback and rejects accidental double-click progression. After an error, replay more slowly; after a second error, reveal/count the relevant objects. Four consecutive first-attempt successes advance one level; two assisted rounds in the last three ease one level. Stages are never removed and slow responses are not penalized. See [visual-math.md](docs/visual-math.md) for deterministic debug overrides.

## UX, art and sound decisions to preserve

- **Primary Colors is the default palette; forward Starfield is the default backdrop.** Black And White is available for early development and suppresses decorative background fields. Alternative palettes and rotating stars/demoscene-style fields are intentional parent controls. Versioned default migrations must not repeatedly overwrite later parent choices.
- Keep the approved ABC branding in [Content/Branding](Content/Branding): primary toy letters on a navy tile, with matching executable, window and taskbar icons. Fredoka and Baloo Bhai 2 are bundled Google fonts with OFL notices. Some classic/UI fonts still depend on Windows Segoe UI/Georgia.
- Letters, numbers and icons use extruded/toon presentation. Non-alphanumeric keys have deterministic, parent-remappable **Font Awesome** identities (F1 defaults to a smile); their screen positions may be random. Smash titles/instructions disappear when typing starts.
- Only **consecutive instances of the same key** inflate one balloon. Default pop scale is 3× and deflation is about three seconds per inflation increment, both configurable. Inflation pulses elastically and rises; another key retires the old balloon. Returning to A creates a new A. This preserves repeated letters in words such as DADDY.
- Liquid blobs have distinct core/boundary colors, merging surfaces and irregular moving edges. Mouse trails use overlapping additive radial gradients; the pointer repels assets, not effect particles. Held Space grows themed fire upward and ignites assets entering it.
- **Left click fires the cannon in both Smash and Word Adventure; right click makes a local blast.** A cannonball continues until its first swept collision or offscreen. The pointer sets direction, not a timed explosion destination.
- Glass cracks stop at existing boundaries, grow under continued pressure and heal when mashing stops. Shattering uses the **same pane geometry** shown by the cracks, rather than unrelated random shards.
- Quantity/math evaluation stays visually still and uncluttered. READY/SET/GO is short; success gets a brief strong volley/burst, errors get neutral instructional feedback. No lives, currency, failure screen or unexplained scoring in these activities. Global mute applies.
- The child-facing picker opens at startup and on two completed, unmodified **G taps within 1.2 seconds**. Large cards support hover/click; arrows/Enter and Tab filters also work. Math Games/back and unmodified Escape return to the picker. Parent controls remain separate. Catalog metadata supports age/type/topic organization without a new registration framework.

Speech is offline/free by default. [Voice.cs](Studio/Voice.cs) uses warmed reusable synthesizers with separate bounded key/word lanes (default four/two). New ordinary speech does not cancel already-playing words. Recordings/prepared WAVs/cache precede immediate Windows speech fallback; optional local Piper prepares future clips in the background without replaying stale work. Seventy prepared clips ship; the full Piper runtime/model does not. Math phase narration is intentionally serialized, cancels obsolete prompts and has bounded waits so a missing audio completion cannot freeze play.

Keep asset licenses: application MIT; Font Awesome notices; font OFL files; CC0 sound attribution in [Content/Sounds/ATTRIBUTION.md](Content/Sounds/ATTRIBUTION.md). Squawks are procedural. Review Piper's GPL runtime redistribution obligations before bundling it; current optional setup is described in [voice.md](docs/voice.md). Effect “plugins” are bounded, **data-only JSON recipes**, not executable code, arbitrary shaders or network extensions.

## Input protection: critical invariants and recovery

The strongest product requirement is resistance to toddler keyboard mashing. The implementation is nevertheless a **Windows session guard, not an OS kiosk**. Fullscreen/topmost/grab, canceled close requests, a single protected-session mutex and a dedicated low-level keyboard hook complement each other. The hook renews every second because Windows can silently remove a timed-out hook. Do not promise that it blocks secure desktops, Ctrl+Alt+Delete, all touchpad gestures, sleep or hardware limitations.

1. **Never capture background input.** Every hook callback checks the actual native game HWND against the foreground window and checks the desktop's `UOI_IO` flag before retaining or suppressing a key. Failed checks pass input to Windows. Focus/desktop changes disarm capture; only the visible game loop rearms it. SDL `Window.Handle` is not a Windows HWND. Background parent shortcuts must also pass through.
2. **Live state is independent of history.** A 256-bit `KeySnapshot` is published separately from the bounded transition queue and replaces gameplay's held set every frame. Gesture/word consumers cannot swallow it. Overflow rebuilds state; events older than 250 ms are discarded, never replayed as delayed speech. Ordinary `GetAsyncKeyState` polling cannot simply replace this ledger: suppressed key delivery can make polling report false releases.
3. **Match physical presses and releases.** Scan code plus extended flag is the identity; Num Lock/Shift can change the virtual-key label while held. Preserve modifier normalization, duplicate virtual-key references (main/keypad Enter), Pause pulses and overrun/synthetic-packet handling. Injected presses cannot authorize parent actions. Tests cover 20 reported simultaneous keys; hardware rollover still limits what the OS receives.
4. **Parent controls read the current exact physical state.** Hold Ctrl+Alt+O **or Ctrl+Shift+O** for two seconds to toggle options; hold Ctrl+Alt+Escape for two seconds to exit. Exactly three physical keys; extras reset the hold. Lock *indicators* do not count as pressed keys. Release all keys before another action; long update gaps reset the hold. Runtime uses `ParentHold` in `KeySnapshot.cs`; the older release-based `ParentChord` class is not the active route.
5. **Preserve emergency tap routes.** Ten completed Escape taps exit; ten completed O taps open options/reset the ledger. Another key-down resets the sequence; auto-repeat is not another tap. These intentionally bypass poisoned historical held state, but only operate while the game owns input.
6. **Visibility changes reset the whole transient session.** [StudioGame.Lifecycle.cs](Studio/StudioGame.Lifecycle.cs) clears native/game key state, queued input, shortcut/gesture/word/count fragments, pointer edges, speech/audio and visible effects on focus, visibility, minimize/restore/maximize transitions. Cancel calibration and replay quantity/math problems appropriately while preserving earned progress. Never wait forever for a key-up that happened on another desktop.
7. **Accessibility changes are temporary.** Disable only Sticky/Filter/Toggle Keys activation-shortcut/confirmation flags, preserving enabled accessibility features. Restore on focus loss/normal exit; a separate watchdog restores after a crash. Do not turn this into permanent OS configuration changes.

## Learning and regressions already addressed

| Failure / superseded approach | Cause and current decision |
| --- | --- |
| Parent chords stopped working; speech continued after returning from another window. | Lost releases, stale event history and key-label changes poisoned held state. Physical snapshots, independent recovery and coordinated focus resets address these separately; do not merge them back into recognizer state. |
| Letters/password input spoke on the lock screen; Caps Lock could not be changed. | Background hook suppression/capture was unsafe. Foreground/native-desktop gating now fails open before collecting input. Actual laptop/secure-desktop acceptance remains necessary. |
| Deliberate typing eventually became paint/glass. | Gesture inference used maximum historical held count and permissive learned overrides. Current overlap is bounded by distinct keys in the last 140 ms; deliberate/rapid classification is deterministic. |
| Gesture training and word habits became entangled. | Current gesture calibration is separate, disabled by default and trained only by parent-labeled 12-second sessions. The tiny 6→8→5 network can only confidently refine a physically plausible BroadMash into Cluster after sufficient samples. Gameplay never trains it. Legacy embedded weights are retired without discarding word habits. |
| Words disappeared during ordinary fast typing. | A deliberate-only recognition gate rejected rapid typing. Both deliberate and rapid input now permit exact words; actual multi-key clusters reset fragments. |
| “mom” interrupted “mommy”, or all short words waited unnaturally. | Blanket prefix delays were abandoned. Per-prefix continuation statistics start immediate, increase the cadence-scaled wait after longer completions and shrink after standalone use. An incompatible next letter resolves the pending shorter word. This is statistics, not a neural net. Unique one-edit typo recovery is bounded; ambiguous corrections are rejected. |
| Voice became delayed/canceled/missed. | A single queue/cancellation policy was unsuitable for smash play. Restore bounded overlapping lanes and immediate fallback; keep diagnostics for overflow. Focus/parent transitions intentionally stop speech. Guided spelling completion stays independent of learned prefix waits. |
| Explorers were sluggish, got stuck high/inverted, showed mirrored letters or clipped roads. | Movement now supports loops/rolls and assisted altitude/orientation recovery; glyph facing was corrected; terrain excludes the road strip. Physical boundaries and model tests exist, but do not substitute for laptop visual/play acceptance. |
| Low-resolution Dot Pop drawing disagreed with pointer hit targets. | Render-target minimum dimensions clamped axes differently. Shared aspect-fit mapping now compensates for that difference. Preserve it when changing resolution systems. |
| QA changed the parent's mode/mute/progress. | Previews formerly reused normal saves. `--preview` without `--data` now creates a unique temporary profile. Explicit `--data` remains writable and must point at a disposable test profile. See the related shortcut regression below. |

Gesture inference estimates cadence, concurrency, spread and trace direction; it does not establish which hand was used. Word learning persists aggregate counts, prefix habits and cadence, **not raw keystroke history**. Gesture training and word history have independent resets. Counting preserves a partial expected number (e.g. 1 then 0 at ten) instead of immediately announcing one.
